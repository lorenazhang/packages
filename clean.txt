

Slide 1 — RCA Overview

Objective

* Develop a multi-signal detection framework to proactively identify emerging customer issues before they develop into broader concerns.
* Combine multiple independent signals to provide stronger evidence for identifying and prioritizing issues for further investigation.

Framework

1. Derive multiple signals from customer interactions and feedback.
2. Establish a 13-month historical baseline for each signal.
3. Apply outlier and trend detection to identify unusual or sustained changes.
4. Use LLMs to identify detailed topics and themes for further investigation.

⸻

Slide 2 — Key Signals

1. Call-to-Complaint Conversion Rate

* Measures how frequently customer calls subsequently result in complaints.
* Calculates overall, service, and escalated complaint counts and conversion rates.
* Analyzed by week × call driver × LOB × sub-LOB.

2. Recontact Rate

* Measures how frequently customers contact the bank again following an initial interaction.
* Provides an additional indicator of unresolved or recurring customer friction.
* Signal: Recontact Calls / Total Calls

3. Social Media

* Leverages Sprinklr data across social platforms, including X, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and Bluesky.
* Provides additional context on customer concerns and emerging themes outside traditional call and complaint channels.
* Data is highly noisy, unstructured, and subjective, with a limited proportion containing clear and actionable issue information.
* Best suited as a corroborating signal rather than a standalone EO signal.

⸻

Slide 3 — Outlier & Trend Detection

Outlier & Trend Detection

Establish a 13-month historical baseline for each signal and compare current performance against historical patterns.

1. Z-Score — Point Anomaly Detection

* Compares the current conversion rate with its historical mean and variability.
* Identifies unusually elevated observations relative to historical behavior.
* Useful for detecting sudden spikes or abrupt changes.

Question answered: “Is the current rate unusually high?”

2. Upward Trend — Sustained Change Detection

* Evaluates whether conversion rates show a consistent upward pattern over recent periods.
* Helps distinguish sustained deterioration from temporary fluctuations.
* Complements Z-score by identifying gradual but persistent increases.

Question answered: “Is the issue consistently getting worse?”

Slide 4 — Key Data Considerations

1. LOB Mapping

Challenge

* Call and complaint data use different business taxonomies.
* Call data is primarily associated with Virtual Queue, while complaint data contains its own LOB classification.

Approach

* Map Virtual Queue to standardized LOB / sub-LOB using the available VQ mapping.
* Use the standardized call-side hierarchy for baseline and signal aggregation.
* Monitor unmapped Virtual Queues and mapping coverage when interpreting results.

Call Data:
Virtual Queue → VQ Mapping → LOB / Sub-LOB

Complaint Data:
Complaint → LOB About Product

⸻

2. Interaction ID Standardization

Challenge

* Interaction identifiers changed during the migration from the legacy call platform to the new system.
* Historical calls primarily use CONNID, while newer interactions may use Global ID.
* Identifier availability varies across the migration period.

Approach

* Apply a hierarchical matching strategy using the best available identifier:
    * CONNID2 → Complaint CONN_ID
    * Global ID → Complaint GLOBAL_ID
    * Historical CONNID → Complaint CONN_ID
* This preserves historical call coverage and ensures a consistent 13-month baseline across the platform migration.
