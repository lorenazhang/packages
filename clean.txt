Slide 1 — Emerging Observations (EO) Overview

Emerging Observations (EO)

Multi-Signal Early Detection of Emerging Customer Issues

Objective
Develop an analytical framework to proactively identify emerging customer issues by combining multiple customer signals and detecting unusual or sustained changes before they develop into broader concerns.

EO Framework
Customer Interactions → Multiple Signals → Historical Baseline → Outlier & Trend Detection → Emerging Observation

Key Principle
An individual signal may not be sufficient to identify an emerging issue. Convergence across multiple independent signals provides stronger evidence for further investigation.

⸻

Slide 2 — EO Signal Framework

Three Key Signals

1. Call-to-Complaint Conversion Rate

* Measures how frequently customer calls subsequently result in complaints.
* Normalizes complaint activity by underlying call volume.
* Helps identify call drivers with disproportionately high complaint outcomes.
* Signal: Complaint-Linked Calls / Total Calls

2. Recontact Rate

* Measures how frequently customers contact the bank again after an initial interaction.
* Provides an indicator of unresolved or recurring customer friction.
* Elevated recontact may indicate that the original issue was not fully resolved.
* Signal: Recontact Calls / Total Calls

3. Social Media

* Uses external customer conversations as an additional qualitative signal.
* Identifies recurring or emerging customer themes across social media platforms.
* Helps determine whether internally detected issues are also appearing in external customer conversations.
* Signal: LLM-Based Theme Identification and Cross-Signal Corroboration

Key Takeaway:
Higher-confidence EO = multiple signals pointing to the same underlying customer issue.

⸻

Slide 3 — Signal Methodology

Call-to-Complaint Conversion Rate

* Identify eligible customer calls.
* Link calls to downstream complaints using interaction identifiers.
* Aggregate by week, call driver, LOB, and sub-LOB.
* Calculate overall, service, and escalated complaint conversion rates.
* Compare current performance against the historical baseline.

Recontact Rate

* Identify recontact relationships at the call level.
* Link recontact records to call-driver data.
* Aggregate by call driver and business hierarchy.
* Calculate recontact count and recontact rate.
* Compare elevated recontact activity with other EO signals.

Social Media

* Source customer messages from Sprinklr across social platforms.
* Filter and profile relevant complaint-related conversations.
* Use LLMs to identify recurring customer issue themes.
* Compare same-period social themes with issues identified from calls and complaints.
* Use social media as a corroborating signal rather than a standalone EO trigger.

⸻

Slide 4 — 13-Month Historical Baseline

Purpose

Establish the historical behavior of each call-driver and business combination so that current activity can be evaluated relative to its own historical pattern.

Baseline Process

13 Months of Call Data
→ Call-to-Complaint Matching
→ Virtual Queue to LOB/Sub-LOB Mapping
→ Weekly Aggregation
→ Historical Baseline

Baseline Granularity

Week × Call Driver × LOB × Sub-LOB

Core Metrics

* Call Count
* Complaint Count
* Service Complaint Count
* Escalated Complaint Count
* Overall Complaint Conversion Rate
* Service Complaint Conversion Rate
* Escalated Complaint Conversion Rate

Why 13 Months?

* Captures approximately one full year of historical behavior.
* Provides sufficient observations for historical comparison.
* Helps account for seasonal patterns and normal variation.

Slide 5 — Outlier & Trend Detection

Z-Score — Point Anomaly Detection

Overview

* Compares the current conversion rate with its historical mean and variability.
* Measures how unusual the current observation is relative to historical behavior.

Interpretation

* A high positive Z-score indicates an unusually elevated conversion rate.
* Useful for identifying sudden spikes or abrupt deterioration.
* Can be combined with minimum volume thresholds to reduce noise.

Question Answered:
“Is the current rate unusually high?”

Trend Detection — Sustained Movement

Overview

* Evaluates whether conversion rates are consistently increasing over recent periods.
* Focuses on sustained deterioration rather than a one-period spike.

Interpretation

* Consistent increases indicate a potential emerging trend.
* Magnitude of rate change can be used to prioritize stronger trends.
* Helps distinguish persistent movement from temporary volatility.

Question Answered:
“Is the issue consistently getting worse?”

Z-Score = Unusual Now | Trend = Consistently Worsening

Slide 6 — From Signal to Emerging Observation

Combining Signals to Increase Confidence

Conversion Rate Anomaly

* Unusually high conversion rate
* High Z-score and/or sustained upward trend

+

Elevated Recontact

* Increased repeat customer contacts
* Potential indication of unresolved customer friction

+

Social Media Corroboration

* Similar customer issue themes appearing in external conversations
* Provides additional evidence that the issue extends beyond internal metrics

= Higher-Confidence Emerging Observation

Example

A call driver shows an unusually high escalated complaint conversion rate, continues increasing across multiple periods, and similar customer concerns appear in recontact and social media data.

Result: Prioritize the issue for further investigation and root-cause analysis.

Key Principle:
EO prioritizes convergence across multiple signals rather than treating any single metric as conclusive.

Slide 7 — Calculation Nuance: LOB Mapping

Calls and Complaints Use Different Business Taxonomies

Call-Side Mapping

Virtual Queue → VQ Mapping → LOB / Sub-LOB

* Call-driver data contains Virtual Queue information.
* Virtual Queues are mapped to standardized LOB and sub-LOB classifications.
* This enables consistent aggregation of call-side signals by business area.
* Mapping coverage is monitored because some Virtual Queues cannot be mapped.

Complaint-Side Classification

Complaint → LOB About Product

* Complaint data uses its own business classification.
* Complaint LOB taxonomy is not always directly comparable to the call-side LOB hierarchy.

Key Consideration

Call LOB Taxonomy ≠ Complaint LOB Taxonomy

Taxonomy and mapping differences must be considered when comparing signals and interpreting business-level trends.

Slide 8 — Calculation Nuance: Interaction ID Migration

Interaction Matching Across Legacy and New Systems

Background

Call identifiers evolved as customer interactions migrated from the legacy call platform to the new system.

Legacy System
CONNID

Migration Period
CONNID + Global ID

New System
Global ID

Challenge

* Historical calls were primarily identified by CONNID.
* CONNID2 became more consistently populated later in the migration.
* Global ID became available as calls migrated to the new platform.
* Historical availability of the newer identifier fields was inconsistent.
* Relying only on newer identifiers would artificially reduce historical call counts.

Final Matching Hierarchy

1. CONNID2 Available
Match call CONNID2 to complaint CONN_ID.

2. CONNID2 Unavailable + Global ID Available
Match call Global ID to complaint GLOBAL_ID.

3. Neither CONNID2 nor Global ID Available
Fall back to the historical CONNID and match to complaint CONN_ID.

Why It Matters

This approach preserves historical call coverage while supporting new-system interactions, enabling consistent 13-month baseline calculations across the platform migration.
